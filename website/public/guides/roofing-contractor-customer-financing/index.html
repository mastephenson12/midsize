How Roofers Can Offer Financing Clearly

Offer payment options with clear scope, transparent costs and lender-approved terms.

By Mark Stephenson, founder of MidSize AI · Published November 13, 2026

The short answer

Choose a financing program by its borrower terms, total merchant cost, funding rules and fit with your roofing workflow. Present the roof scope and cash price clearly, then let the homeowner review the lender's actual offer. Approval is the lender's decision.

What does offering financing cost the roofer?

  • Fixed costs: subscription, onboarding, software or integration charges.
  • Transaction costs: dealer fees, promotional-plan charges and settlement deductions.
  • Operational costs: staff time, delayed funds, cancellation handling and change orders.
Fictional cost example: a hypothetical 4% merchant fee on a $15,000 financed transaction is $600, leaving $14,400 before other costs. This is not a provider quote. A fixed annual subscription has a different cost pattern; compare it against realistic funded volume, not just applications.

Do not assume you can add a financing fee to the customer price. Ask the provider about permitted pricing and disclosures and obtain appropriate advice for your agreement and location. Make the written cash price, financed project price and lender terms clear.

A clear customer-financing workflow

  1. Agree on the roof scope. Present work, materials, exclusions, change-order process and cash project price before discussing a payment.
  2. Offer a choice. Explain that financing is optional and customers may compare an independent bank or credit union.
  3. Use the provider's secure application. Let the borrower enter sensitive information there. Do not collect Social Security numbers, bank credentials or credit applications through an ordinary lead form.
  4. Separate prequalification from approval. Use the lender's exact language. Do not promise approval, a rate, a payment or “no credit impact” beyond the verified stage and terms.
  5. Review the actual offer. Direct customers to APR, fees, term, total payments, collateral and promotional conditions. Use lender-approved materials and ask the provider to approve required advertising disclosures.
  6. Confirm authorization and funding. Follow the agreement's deposit, stage or completion rules. Never ask the customer to certify completion before the applicable work is complete.
  7. Handle changes explicitly. Confirm whether a new application, amended loan or separate payment is needed before extra work or cancellation.

For promotions, distinguish true zero-interest offers from deferred interest. The CFPB's explanation shows why “no interest if paid in full” deserves careful reading. The borrower's agreement controls the actual offer.

A plain-English introduction your team can use

Here is the written scope and cash price. If you prefer to explore monthly payments, we can share our financing provider's secure link. You can also compare your bank or credit union. The lender decides approval and terms. Please review the APR, fees, payment schedule, total cost and any promotional deadline before accepting.

Questions to ask each financing provider

  • Which states, roofing businesses, job types and project sizes are eligible?
  • Who is the actual lender and who receives the funds?
  • What are every fixed, transaction and promotional-plan charge?
  • What happens at prequalification, final application and credit reporting?
  • What borrower APR, term, fee and collateral options are available?
  • When can we request deposits or progress payments, and what must the customer authorize?
  • What happens with disputes, canceled jobs, refunds and change orders?
  • Which website language, texts, payment examples and disclosures are approved?
  • Can the program connect to our actual estimating and CRM tools, and what customer data is shared?

Track customer understanding, complaints and successful completion as well as applications and funded volume. Do not make a larger loan or higher-priced roof the only measure of success.

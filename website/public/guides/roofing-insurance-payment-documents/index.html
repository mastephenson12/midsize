Roofing Insurance Payments: Find the Bottleneck

Organize the next action for insurer review, depreciation, mortgage release and homeowner payment.

By Mark Stephenson, founder of MidSize AI · Published November 13, 2026

The short answer

Identify who controls the next payment and what document or action is missing. Insurer review, recoverable depreciation, mortgage-company release and the homeowner's unpaid invoice are separate stages. Organizing the file can reduce avoidable delays; no checklist guarantees a payout or a payment date.

Insurance proceeds are not necessarily paid directly to the roofer. Check actual payees and authorization. The California regulator's claims guide describes mortgage-company inspections and release issues. Use it for process context, not as a source of deadlines in another state.

Do not assume all depreciation is recoverable

Some replacement-cost claims start with an actual-cash-value payment and release additional eligible funds after repair evidence. ACV-only or roof-specific terms may differ. The homeowner should confirm the policy's requirements and deadlines with the insurer. See the NAIC claims guide.

Build a factual, organized document packet

  • File reference: job reference, claim reference where authorized, property and approved contacts. Share only necessary information through approved channels.
  • Damage record: dated photos with locations and captions; original files retained.
  • Measurements and scope: roof sections, quantities, units, specific materials and work.
  • Disputed work explanation: identify each item and attach relevant photos, product instructions or applicable official code references. Do not assert unverified requirements.
  • Actual costs and completion: signed changes, accurate invoices, receipts, completion photos and requested inspection records.
  • Submission log: document version, date sent, recipient, receipt confirmation, open question and next follow-up.

Send one organized packet rather than repeated unlabelled attachments. Ask the recipient to confirm receipt and identify any remaining requirement. A paid invoice, completion certificate or deductible receipt must accurately reflect what happened.

A factual status request

With the homeowner's authorization, we submitted the attached construction documents on [date]. Please confirm receipt and identify any specific missing information for the next review or payment stage. Which items remain disputed, who is handling them, and when should the homeowner expect the next status update?

When a file still is not moving

  1. Confirm the correct recipient and request a written status and missing-items list.
  2. Have the policyholder escalate to the insurer's claims supervisor or customer-relations team.
  3. For a mortgage hold, use the mortgage servicer's loss-draft process; insurer escalation will not necessarily release those funds.
  4. For an unresolved insurance-handling concern, the policyholder can contact the applicable state insurance department. In Arizona, use DIFI's complaint process.
  5. For coverage disputes, contract collection or legal remedies, obtain appropriate licensed professional advice rather than promising a result.
Arizona-specific timing: A.R.S. § 20-462 contains interest provisions for certain first-party claims after receipt of acceptable proof of loss containing the information needed for adjudication. Exceptions and recipient requirements apply, including good-faith denials. It is not a blanket 30-day deadline for every roofing invoice. Ask a qualified professional whether it applies before asserting a legal entitlement.

Choose supplement and claim services carefully

Preparing a construction estimate and negotiating an insurance settlement are different activities. Check the provider's actual work, applicable licensing, fees, data handling and authority. Arizona DIFI's licensing guidance explains adjuster requirements and exceptions. Do not assume a roofing license or software subscription authorizes coverage negotiation.

Do not sign or endorse checks for homeowners without proper authority, misstate work completion, inflate invoices or rely on a blanket assignment as a shortcut. Explain payment milestones before starting and follow the actual contract and funding rules.

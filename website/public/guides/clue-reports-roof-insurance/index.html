CLUE Reports and Roof Claims: What Homeowners Should Know

Understand your claims history and ask better questions about coverage, renewal and payment.

By Mark Stephenson, founder of MidSize AI · Published November 13, 2026

The short answer

A CLUE report records insurance claim history. It is not a rule that three claims in five years automatically disqualify a damaged roof from coverage. Claims can affect underwriting, pricing and renewal. Payment for a specific loss is a separate question governed by the policy, cause of damage and claim facts.

Your CLUE report · Claim history versus coverage · Questions versus claims · Next steps

What is a CLUE report, and how do I get mine?

CLUE means Comprehensive Loss Underwriting Exchange. LexisNexis collects up to seven years of home and personal-property claims information for insurance underwriting and pricing. You can request one free report every 12 months. Requesting your own report does not hurt your credit scores. See the CFPB's CLUE resource.

What should I check?

  • Your identity and property address.
  • Loss dates, claim types and payment amounts.
  • Claims that belong to someone else or appear duplicated.
  • Whether the reported status and payment match your records.

A claim can appear even if denied or unpaid. The Washington insurance regulator explains how opened, denied and paid claims may be reported. Not all insurers participate, so absence from a report is not proof that a loss never occurred.

If information is wrong or incomplete, dispute it with LexisNexis and the insurer that supplied it. Keep copies of your evidence and dispute correspondence. The CFPB explains the right to a free investigation of disputed information. Do not pay someone who promises to erase accurate claims.

Do three claims in five years mean insurance will not pay for my roof?

Do not treat that claim count as a universal cutoff. An insurer's underwriting rules and state restrictions may affect future availability or pricing. They do not replace the coverage terms for a particular loss under an existing policy.

If an insurer takes adverse action based on a consumer report, the notice identifies the reporting agency. You may be entitled to another free report and can dispute errors. See the CFPB explanation of insurance reports and adverse action.

Replacement cost and actual cash value are different

Actual cash value generally reflects depreciation. Replacement-cost coverage may allow additional payment after repair or replacement requirements are met. Roof-specific endorsements can differ from the rest of the home's coverage. Ask which terms apply to your roof, whether depreciation is recoverable, and what proof and deadlines apply. The NAIC explains the valuation distinction.

Does asking about coverage count as filing a claim?

A coverage inquiry and an actual claim are different. Say clearly what you intend and ask the representative to confirm how the contact is recorded. Describing an actual loss and asking the company to investigate can be different from a general policy question.

Arizona-specific: A.R.S. § 20-1652(F) protects mere coverage inquiries from being treated as claims or reported as inquiries to insurance-support or consumer-reporting organizations. This is an Arizona rule; other states have their own requirements.

Do not use an inquiry label to conceal a loss or avoid policy duties. Check notice deadlines, document damage and take appropriate steps to prevent further damage. Ask your insurer what emergency-repair documentation it needs. Arizona DIFI's homeowner resource discusses these responsibilities and claim-history considerations.

A practical homeowner checklist

  1. Locate the policy, declarations and roof endorsements.
  2. Document the damage and necessary emergency work without putting yourself at risk.
  3. Ask about reporting deadlines and required evidence promptly; do not wait for a CLUE report if that could jeopardize notice duties.
  4. Request your report and dispute factual errors.
  5. Ask your agent about underwriting implications and your insurer about this loss's coverage.
  6. Get a written roofing scope and compare it with the insurer's covered scope.
  7. Keep the claim decision, invoices, receipts and payment-release instructions together.

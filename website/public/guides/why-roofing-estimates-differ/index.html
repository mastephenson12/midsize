Behind the estimate

Why two roofers can quote different prices for the same roof.

The measurements may match. The work, products, assumptions and business pricing may not. Here is how to compare what you are actually buying.

The short answer

Two roofers can price the same property differently because they included different work, quantities, products, labor assumptions, exclusions, warranties or business costs. Software organizes those decisions; it does not make every proposal equivalent. Compare written scope before choosing by total alone.

A polished proposal is not proof of workmanship. A higher price is not proof of excessive profit. Ask the contractor to explain the decisions in writing rather than trying to infer intentions from the document.

How does a roofer turn measurements into a price?

  1. Measure the roof. Establish roof area, slopes, edges and penetrations. House floor area and roof surface area are different measurements.
  2. Decide the work. Specify removal, replacement, components to reuse, protection, cleanup and exclusions. Measurements alone do not establish hidden conditions.
  3. Calculate quantities. Translate measurements into materials, with allowances for cuts and waste. Starter, ridge materials and accessories may be separate.
  4. Estimate the effort. Account for access, steepness, existing layers, equipment, disposal and the proposed installation method.
  5. Set the selling price. Apply company pricing, business costs and profit assumptions, then present the scope, options, warranty and payment terms.

This is an explanatory framework, not a claim that every contractor uses the same formula. Verisk's pricing research methodology explains that complexity, access, location, overhead and service can affect prices.

Why can the material quantity exceed the roof area?

In an illustrative example, 2,000 square feet equals 20 roofing squares because one square represents 100 square feet. A hypothetical 10% allowance makes 22 squares of coverage material. That is arithmetic, not a recommended waste factor for your roof. Ask how the allowance was calculated and whether starter and ridge quantities are separate. Extra quantity alone does not establish overcharging.

Measurement reports describe geometry; they do not by themselves establish concealed damage or the full work required. Likewise, a proposal template does not reveal a contractor's private costs or actual profit.

For an insurance-related project, keep the documents distinct. Ask which document is the contractor's offer and which is the claim estimate. Do not assume a claim estimate is a signed contractor agreement or a guarantee of payment. Ask your insurer about coverage and claim-payment questions; ask the contractor to clarify the proposed work.

Financing can also change how offers look. Ask for the cash project price and the separate financing disclosures. A monthly payment alone does not show total borrowing cost.

A fair question to send each roofer

Thanks for the proposal. I am comparing the written work, not just the totals. Could you explain the measured roof area, material allowance, exact products, components being reused, exclusions and extra-charge rules? Please also confirm the written warranty, payment milestones and what changes between your package options. An updated scope would help me compare the proposals fairly.

Lead response SOP 02

New Web-Lead Response SOP

Turn every new form, chat, and advertising inquiry into an acknowledged lead with one owner and one dated next action.

For: Roofing, HVAC, plumbing, electrical & home servicesVersion: 1.0Published: September 14, 2026Review every 90 days

Purpose and ownership

Speed matters, but ownership prevents the real leak.

This SOP begins when a prospect submits a website form, starts a chat, requests information through an advertisement, or sends another digital inquiry. The goal is to acknowledge the person quickly, validate the request, and place it with the right human before it becomes another unread notification.

Primary ownerCSR, dispatcher, office manager, or named sales coordinator.
Response targetAutomatic acknowledgement within 1 minute; human review and first attempt within 5 minutes during coverage hours.
Done meansThe inquiry has a source, status, owner, contact preference, and dated next action—or a documented closed outcome.

The first five minutes

Capture → Confirm → Route → Nurture

Capture the complete inquiry

Create one lead record with name, phone, email, source/campaign, submission time, requested service, property ZIP code, message, and stated contact preference. Preserve the original message.

Confirm receipt within one minute

Send a truthful acknowledgement through the channel the person requested or used. Confirm receipt without claiming that an appointment, price, technician, or service availability has been approved.

Validate and deduplicate

Check for required contact information, obvious spam, an existing customer record, and another open lead from the same person. Merge duplicates so two employees do not contact the same homeowner independently.

Assign one accountable owner

Route by service type, ZIP code, urgency, lead source, existing-customer status, and team capacity. If routing fails, send the lead to a visible exception queue owned by a named person—not a shared inbox with no deadline.

Make the first human contact attempt

Review the submitted details before calling or replying. Ask only for missing information. Use the person’s stated contact preference and give urgent or sensitive situations to a qualified human.

Set the next action before leaving the record

Record the attempt, outcome, next channel, next contact time, and owner. Stop follow-up after an opt-out, wrong number, confirmed duplicate/spam, out-of-scope request, or another documented stop condition.

Coverage-hours rule: define the exact hours when a five-minute human attempt is realistic. After hours, acknowledge receipt, apply approved urgent escalation rules, and state when the team will review the request. Never imply 24/7 human coverage unless it actually exists.

Copyable scripts

Respond like a helpful local company, not a software sequence.

Immediate acknowledgement

Hi [First name], thanks for contacting [Company] about [service]. We received your request. A member of our team will review it [during coverage hours / by stated time]. If anything is actively leaking, unsafe, or without essential service, reply URGENT and briefly describe what is happening.

First human text

Hi [First name], this is [Name] with [Company]. I’m reviewing your request for [service]. I have the property in [ZIP/city]. Before we discuss the next step, is there anything urgent we should know, and is text or a phone call easier for you?

No-response follow-up

Hi [First name], [Name] from [Company] following up on your [service] request. If you still need help, reply here with the best time to reach you. If you already handled it or do not want more follow-up, let me know and I’ll close the request.
Consent and preference matter. A submitted form is not permission for unlimited outreach. Honor channel preferences and opt-outs, identify the company clearly, and use only the cadence your business has approved for that lead source.

Routing and exceptions

Every branch needs a visible destination.

Measurement

Measure from submission to a real human outcome.

SpeedMedian acknowledgement time and median first-human-attempt time by source.
OwnershipPercentage assigned within five minutes; number left in the exception queue past its deadline.
OutcomeContact, qualified, appointment, sold-job, opt-out, duplicate, and spam rates by source.

Review sources separately. A fast response to low-quality spam is not success, and a high appointment rate does not prove profit. Compare lead quality, close rate, collected revenue, and workload before changing ad spend or automations.

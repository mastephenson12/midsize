Missed-Lead Recovery SOP

Recover missed calls and digital inquiries with one accountable process—from the first 60-second acknowledgement through the final respectful follow-up.

For: Roofing, HVAC, plumbing, electrical & home servicesVersion: 1.1Published: September 14, 2026Review every 90 days
Jump to Checklist

Purpose and ownership

Recover the conversation, not merely the phone call.

This SOP begins when a new or existing customer calls and no person answers. The goal is to acknowledge the caller quickly, capture enough context to act, and place the opportunity with one accountable human owner.

Primary ownerCSR, dispatcher, office manager, or named on-call employee.
First-response targetAutomated acknowledgement within 1 minute; human review within 5 minutes during coverage hours.
Done meansThe caller has an owner, status, next action, and scheduled follow-up—or a documented closed outcome.

The operating procedure

Capture → Confirm → Route → Nurture

Capture the missed call

Record caller number, time, source or tracking number, new-versus-existing status when known, and whether a voicemail was left. Do not let the call exist only in a phone notification.

Confirm receipt immediately

Send the acknowledgement text below within one minute. It should confirm that the message reached the company without pretending that a technician or appointment is already available.

Assign one human owner

During coverage hours, the designated owner reviews the call within five minutes. After hours, the on-call owner handles urgent categories; all other calls receive a dated next action for the next coverage window.

Qualify and route

Identify the service requested, property ZIP code, urgency, active damage or safety concern, ownership/authorization, and preferred callback time. Route by trade, service area, urgency, and appointment capacity.

Nurture when the caller does not respond

Make the first human call, leave a voicemail when appropriate, and send the follow-up text. Try again at the agreed cadence. Stop when the person opts out, says no, is outside scope, or another documented stop condition applies.

Record the outcome

Use one status: contacted, appointment scheduled, follow-up scheduled, outside service area, not a fit, duplicate/spam, existing-customer issue, or opted out. Every open lead must have a named owner and dated next action.

Copyable scripts

Use plain language that earns the reply.

Immediate acknowledgement text

Hi [First name], this is [Company]. Sorry we missed your call. We received it and a member of our team will review your request. What service do you need, what ZIP code is the property in, and is anything actively leaking, unsafe, or without essential service?

Human callback voicemail

Hi [First name], this is [Name] with [Company], returning your call about [known reason]. I’m sorry we missed you. You can call or text us at [number]. I’ll also send a short text so you have an easy way to reply.

Follow-up after no answer

Hi [First name], [Name] from [Company] here. I just tried returning your call. If you still need help, reply with the service needed, property ZIP code, and the best time to reach you. If you already found help, just let me know and I’ll close the request.
Do not promise what has not been confirmed. Avoid claiming that a technician is dispatched, an appointment is booked, insurance will pay, pricing is final, or an emergency can be handled until a qualified person confirms it.

Day-two email

Subject: Still need help with [service]?

Hi [First name], we tried reaching you after your request to [Company]. If you still need help, reply with the property ZIP code, a short description of the issue, and the best time to call. If the situation has changed or you found help, tell us and we will close the request.

Close-the-loop message

Hi [First name], we have not been able to reach you, so we will close this request for now. If you still need help with [service], reply here or call [number] and we will reopen it. Take care — [Company].

Seven-day recovery cadence

Be persistent enough to help—not persistent enough to become spam.

Use this as a starting cadence, then adapt it to the customer’s channel preference, your coverage hours, applicable consent rules, and the urgency of the request. Stop immediately for opt-outs, wrong numbers, closed requests, or confirmed non-fit leads.

TimingActionOwner and goal
0–1 minuteSend the approved acknowledgement text. For web leads, confirm the request was received.Automation or CSR: earn a reply and collect minimum context.
Within 5 minutesReview the lead, check urgency, call once, and leave the approved voicemail when appropriate.Named human owner: establish contact or a dated next action.
2–4 hoursSend a short, useful text asking for service, ZIP code, and preferred callback time.Owner: make replying easy without repeating the full pitch.
Next business morningCall again, then send the day-two email when email permission and an address are available.Owner: offer two specific callback or appointment windows.
Day 3One channel-appropriate follow-up with a useful next step or preparation tip.Owner: add value; do not send “just checking in.”
Day 5Final live-call attempt during a different time window.Owner or backup: verify whether help is still needed.
Day 7Send the close-the-loop message and close or move the record to a permitted long-term nurture status.Owner: leave the door open and eliminate orphaned leads.

Human handoff rules

Automation acknowledges and organizes. People make judgment calls.

SituationRequired routeDo not do
Active leak, flooding, electrical smell, gas concern, fire, collapse, medical risk, or immediate dangerPresent the company’s approved safety language and escalate immediately to the on-call human. For imminent danger, direct the caller to emergency services.Do not diagnose, minimize danger, or promise arrival.
Existing customer, warranty, complaint, or active jobRoute to the person responsible for the existing relationship with full context.Do not treat it as a fresh sales lead or restart qualification.
New estimate or service request inside scopeCollect minimum details and route to scheduling or the responsible estimator/dispatcher.Do not invent availability, pricing, or technical conclusions.
Outside service area or scopeMark the reason clearly and use an approved referral response if one exists.Do not leave the lead open or repeatedly contact them.
Opt-out, wrong number, spam, or duplicateApply the appropriate stop status immediately.Do not continue automated follow-up.

Measurement

Track whether the process protects revenue.

SpeedMedian time to acknowledgement and median time to human response.
CoveragePercentage of missed calls assigned to an owner with a dated next action.
OutcomeContact rate, appointment rate, sold-job rate, opt-out rate, and unresolved missed calls.

Review a small sample weekly. Check message accuracy, correct routing, opt-out compliance, and whether urgent calls reached a person. Improve the weakest step before adding more lead volume.

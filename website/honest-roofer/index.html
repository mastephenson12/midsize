A calmer decision process

Repair, replace, or get another opinion?

No webpage can diagnose your roof. This four-part process helps you collect better evidence and ask for a written explanation.

Document

Photograph leaks, stains, missing material, dates, weather, and earlier repairs.

Inspect

Ask what was inspected, what could not be seen, and whether photos support the recommendation.

Compare

Compare equivalent scopes and ask why each contractor recommends repair or replacement.

Verify

Check credentials, insurance, references, contract terms, warranties, and permit requirements.

Quick answers

Homeowner roofing FAQ

How many roofing estimates should I get?

For a major repair or replacement, two or three detailed written estimates can expose differences in scope, material, warranties, and exclusions. For active leaks, protect the home first and document emergency work.

Should I choose the lowest bid?

Price matters, but only after confirming that the bids cover comparable work. A lower estimate may exclude decking, flashing, ventilation, permits, cleanup, or warranty coverage included elsewhere.

Can a roofer promise that insurance will pay?

A contractor can document damage and explain repair scope, but your insurer determines coverage under your policy. Be cautious about guaranteed outcomes or requests to hide or misstate costs.

When can a roof coating make sense?

Coatings may extend service life on compatible roof systems that are dry, sound, and properly prepared. The product and application must match the roof; coating over trapped moisture or failed materials does not correct the underlying problem.

What belongs in the contract?

Look for scope, materials, quantities or measurement basis, removal and disposal, flashing, ventilation, decking allowances, permits, schedule, cleanup, payment terms, change-order rules, warranties, and exclusions.
